What does "domesticated" really mean?

To be considered domesticated, an animal must experience both behavioural and physical changes — they grow tamer and often evolve to be smaller than the wild animal in some way.

The reality behind the cuteness

Wild foxes living near humans face serious risks — and so do the people who try to keep them.

1,500+

The number of wild foxes rescued and rehabilitated every year in the UK by The Fox Project, founded by Trevor Williams.

Many foxes suffer from injuries, mange, and human-related dangers.

50%

DeFisher estimates that, in the U.S., at least half of all "pet" foxes bought from the exotic trade end up displaced, euthanised, or illegally released.

"Cute videos" hide a much darker reality for the animals involved.

What does the research say?

Two recent studies suggest foxes may once have been kept as pets...

Study 1 · 2025

Fuegian Dogs of South America

A 2025 study found that indigenous South American cultures kept semi-tame pet foxes known as Fuegian Dogs.

📚 Source: Journal of Zoology (Wiley)
Study 2 · Archaeology

Dusicyon avus

Archaeologists found a now-extinct fox species, Dusicyon avus, buried with its human — suggesting that man's best friend may once have been a fox.

📚 Source: Royal Society Open Science
Russian Experiment

60 years of selective breeding

The only record of truly domesticated foxes comes from a controversial Russian genetics experiment — almost 60 years of selective breeding produced foxes that licked the hand of experimenters and wagged their tails when humans approached.

⚗ A unique scientific case — not natural domestication
